7 Nov 2026 · Bitcoin · 4H and 2H · 10 years of data

When to trade Bitcoin: the busiest hours are also the stop-loss trap

I studied ten years of Bitcoin price data (Nov 2016 to Nov 2026, Bitstamp, about 87,000 hourly bars) and grouped it into 4-hour and 2-hour candles by Indian time. For each candle I measured trading volume, the average move, and how often a simple breakout trade got stopped out. The test: buy a break of the previous candle's high (or sell a break of its low), stop-loss 1 ATR away, target twice the risk.

Busiest, and most stops hit
17:30–21:30 IST

US data at 19:00 and the US open at 20:00. Highest volume (142) but 1 in 6 breakout trades is stopped out within 4 hours.

Best balance
After 21:30 IST

The US-open spike is over, direction is set, volume is still above average. +0.09R per trade when trading with the trend.

Fewest stops hit
09:30–13:30 IST

Quietest volume, smallest moves, but only 5% of stops hit within 4 hours and the best result (+0.11R).

Avoid
01:30–05:30 IST

The only window with a negative result on both 4H and 2H charts.

Volume: 100 = average share of the day's volume. Stop hit inside candle: share of breakout trades whose 1 ATR stop was hit before that candle closed. Result: average profit in R (risk units) of trend-following breakouts (price on the right side of the 50 EMA) with a 2R target, held up to 48 hours. Green rows = best windows, red rows = most stops hit or a negative result.

What I take from this

  • High volume is not the same as easy trading. The US session moves the most, but it also shakes out the most stops. Don't enter in the first 15–30 minutes after 19:00 or 20:00 IST. Let the first candle close, then trade its break.
  • Tight stops lose. A 0.5 ATR stop was hit within 4 hours in 42% of trades. A 1 ATR stop: 10%.
  • The best stop was below the previous 4H candle's low (above its high for shorts), and only in the direction of the 4H trend. That combination returned about +0.10R per trade. Against the trend: −0.10R.
  • Weekends are thin. Saturday and Sunday trade about half the volume of weekdays, so breakouts fail more often.

These edges are small (a few tenths of R) and exclude fees and slippage. This is a timing and risk filter, not a complete trading system. Past patterns do not guarantee future results.
